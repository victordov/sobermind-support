SoberMind Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 2026

SoberMind is built private by design.

What stays on your iPhone

Everything you enter — your quit date, check-ins, journal notes, moods, urges, slips, triggers, reasons, goals, and settings — is stored on your device, in the app's local database. SoberMind never sends any of it to us or to anyone else. There are no accounts.

What is sent, and to whom

Two services receive data from the app. Neither gets your name, your email or anything you wrote or logged.

Anonymous usage, with PostHog. To learn which parts of the app help, SoberMind sends short usage events to PostHog, its analytics provider: which main screens you open; how far you got through the first-launch setup; that a check-in was saved, an urge logged, the SOS screen opened, Protect Tonight started or a lesson completed; and the steps of a purchase (the plans shown, a purchase started, finished or canceled). App opens are counted too. Each event carries a random ID created on your device, plus the app version, iOS version and device model.

Events never contain what you wrote or logged: no journal text, no moods, urge levels or triggers, no drinks, slips or amounts, no reasons, no names and no Apple Health data. A slip is never reported at all. Your location isn't recorded and nothing on your screen is recorded. You can turn this off at any time in Settings → Share Anonymous Usage; nothing more is sent after that.

Purchases, with RevenueCat. Purchases are made and charged by Apple. SoberMind sends the App Store record of a purchase (the plan, when it was bought, its price and whether it renews) to RevenueCat, which checks it and gives us subscription figures. RevenueCat files it under a random ID, not under you, and we never receive payment details. While usage sharing is on, RevenueCat is given the random usage ID, so subscription events such as a renewal or a cancellation can appear next to the usage events.

Neither service is used for advertising. Nothing is sold, and nothing is used to track you across other companies' apps or websites. Their own policies: PostHog and RevenueCat.

Your device backups

If you back up your iPhone with iCloud Backup or to a computer, those backups include SoberMind's data, as they do for your other apps. They belong to you and are protected by your Apple Account or your computer; we can't see them.

Apple Health

If you choose to connect Apple Health, SoberMind reads your sleep and step data on your device only, to show how rest and movement relate to your cravings. Health data is never transmitted anywhere, never used for advertising, and never shared with anyone, in line with Apple's HealthKit requirements.

Purchases

Subscriptions and purchases are processed by Apple. We receive no payment details and no personal information from your purchase; the record that reaches RevenueCat is described above.

Sharing and exports

Apart from the two services above, something leaves your device only when you share it — a milestone card, a progress message, or a backup or export file you create yourself. You choose where those go.

Notifications

Reminders are scheduled locally on your device. With Discreet Notifications enabled, lock-screen wording avoids any mention of sobriety.

Data deletion

Settings → Erase All Data deletes everything SoberMind keeps on your device, and deleting the app does the same. Your entries are on no server, so we hold no copy. Erasing also starts a new random usage ID, so later usage isn't connected to earlier usage; events already sent carry only the old ID and none of your entries. Purchase records stay with Apple, and with RevenueCat for our subscription accounting. Copies you made yourself are yours to delete: backup or export files you shared, and your device backups (iCloud Backup or your computer), which keep the app's data until they are replaced or deleted.

Not medical advice

SoberMind is a self-tracking and educational tool, not medical care. Stopping heavy drinking abruptly can cause serious withdrawal symptoms — if you feel shaky, confused, or unwell, contact a doctor or your local emergency service.
