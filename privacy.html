SoberMind Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 2026

SoberMind is built private by design.

What we collect

Nothing. SoberMind has no accounts, no analytics, no tracking, and no servers. Everything you enter — your quit date, check-ins, journal notes, moods, urges, slips, triggers, goals, and settings — is stored on your device, in the app's local database. SoberMind never sends it to us or to anyone else.

Your device backups

If you back up your iPhone with iCloud Backup or to a computer, those backups include SoberMind's data, as they do for your other apps. They belong to you and are protected by your Apple Account or your computer; we can't see them.

Apple Health

If you choose to connect Apple Health, SoberMind reads your sleep and step data on your device only, to show how rest and movement relate to your cravings. Health data is never transmitted anywhere, never used for advertising, and never shared with anyone, in line with Apple's HealthKit requirements.

Purchases

Subscriptions and purchases are processed entirely by Apple. We receive no payment details and no personal information from your purchase.

Sharing and exports

SoberMind itself sends nothing anywhere. Something leaves your device only when you share it — a milestone card, a progress message, or a backup or export file you create yourself. You choose where those go.

Notifications

Reminders are scheduled locally on your device. With Discreet Notifications enabled, lock-screen wording avoids any mention of sobriety.

Data deletion

Settings → Erase All Data deletes everything SoberMind keeps on your device, and deleting the app does the same. SoberMind has no servers, so we hold no copy. Copies you made yourself are yours to delete: backup or export files you shared, and your device backups (iCloud Backup or your computer), which keep the app's data until they are replaced or deleted.

Contact

Not medical advice

SoberMind is a self-tracking and educational tool, not medical care. Stopping heavy drinking abruptly can cause serious withdrawal symptoms — if you feel shaky, confused, or unwell, contact a doctor or your local emergency service.
